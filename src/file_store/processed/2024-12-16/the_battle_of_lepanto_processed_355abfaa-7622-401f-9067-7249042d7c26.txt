The Battle of Lepanto (October 7, 1571) was a major naval victory for the Holy League (a coalition of Catholic states) over the Ottoman Empire.  The battle, fought primarily with galleys and galleasses, was the largest naval battle in Western history since antiquity.  While the Ottomans lost a significant portion of their fleet, they quickly rebuilt it, diminishing the battle's long-term strategic impact. However, Lepanto held immense symbolic importance for Christian Europe, boosting morale and marking a turning point in the perception of Ottoman power.  It is often compared to the Battle of Salamis for its tactical significance and impact on the course of the religious wars in Europe.  The victory solidified Philip II of Spain's position and prompted Pope Pius V to institute a new feast day.
