The Battle of Lepanto (October 7, 1571) was a major naval victory for the Holy League (a coalition of Catholic states) over the Ottoman Empire.  The battle, fought primarily with galleys and galleasses, was the largest naval battle in Western history since antiquity. While the Ottomans suffered a near-total destruction of their fleet, they quickly rebuilt it, so the battle's military impact was short-lived.  However, Lepanto held significant symbolic importance, boosting Christian morale and undermining the Ottomans' mystique of invincibility in a time of religious conflict.  Historians view it as more of a moral victory than a strategically decisive one for Christendom.
